01

TMS–EEG · Visual working memory

Parietal and Motor Cortical Contributions to Visual Working Memory

How do parietal and motor cortical regions contribute to visual working memory under different states of attentional prioritization?

Using a retro-cue working-memory task, I designed an online TMS study targeting the right posterior parietal cortex and primary motor cortex. An exploratory behavioral study revealed distinct response-time patterns following stimulation at the two sites, with preliminary evidence of cue-dependent changes in recall precision after motor-cortex stimulation. Concurrent TMS–EEG experiments are now examining the corresponding neural dynamics.

Approach
Retro-cue task, online TMS, concurrent TMS–EEG
Status
Behavioral study completed; TMS–EEG data collection in progress
02

Physical activity · Visuomotor integration

Visuomotor Integration Moderates the Effect of Physical Activity on Visual Working Memory

Does visuomotor functioning change the relationship between habitual physical activity and visual working memory?

I developed this project from earlier findings linking habitual exercise to working-memory precision. Robust regression and moderation analyses showed that working-memory-guided visuomotor error moderated the association between moderate-to-vigorous physical activity and working-memory capacity. The result suggests that visuomotor functioning may define an important individual boundary condition in the physical activity–cognition relationship.

Approach
Physical activity assessment, visuomotor tasks, visual working memory, robust moderation analysis
Output
First-author manuscript submitted
03

Perceptual learning · EEG

Format-Specific Processing Demands Determine Transfer of Perceptual Learning

Does transfer-related plasticity depend on the processing demands of a task?

Participants trained on line- or dot-based orientation discrimination. Dot training produced more complete behavioral transfer, whereas line training produced partial transfer. Tensor decomposition revealed distinct neural patterns for direct readout and relational integration, alongside training-related alpha-band changes with different temporal and spatial profiles. Together, the findings indicate that transfer-related plasticity depends on format-specific processing demands.

Approach
Orientation discrimination training, EEG, time–frequency analysis, tensor decomposition
Output
First-author manuscript submitted; oral presentation at ESCoP–CoPM 2026

Previous research

Master’s thesis · fNIRS hyperscanning

Attentional Allocation in Cooperative Tasks Among Athletes

I developed a dyadic paradigm adapted from multiple-object tracking. Cooperative performance increased functional connectivity and inter-brain synchrony in the prefrontal cortex and right temporoparietal junction compared with parallel task performance.

Eye tracking · Dynamic vision · 2023–2024

Goal-Directed Attention in Dynamic Visual Tracking

I contributed to experiments on goal-directed tracking. Eye-movement patterns shifted from predominantly central fixation to alternating fixations between targets as the number of tracked objects increased.
